# Anime Timeline — TECH-LEVEL ORDERING (alternate view, 2026-10-28)

Companion to the corrected chronological timeline. Same shows, different spine:
instead of *when* a story is set, this version sorts by *what its world can
build*. That was the hidden principle in the original list — and it reveals
something the year-order hides.

## The question this answers

Alice's question: does the tech-level pattern mean history was *reset* — a
society rising high, then starting over after a catastrophe — or *hidden*,
like in One Piece? Or is each show just set at its tech level from the
beginning?

Short answer: all three happen in this list, and you can tell them apart.
The tags below mark which one each show is doing:

- **[Collapse]** — a high-tech past fell apart; the present lives in its ruins
  or below its level, and society restarted lower.
- **[Hidden]** — someone deliberately erased, suppressed, or rewrote the past.
- **[Setting]** — neither. The tech level is just when/where the story is set.

## THE RESET MAP (the pattern you spotted)

**Collapse / restart — the world fell and started over:**
Attack on Titan, Gurren Lagann, Ergo Proxy, Akira, Texhnolyze, Darling in
the FranXX, Blue Submarine No. 6, Aquarion, Diebuster, Vampire Hunter D,
Now and Then Here and There, Expelled from Paradise, Eureka Seven,
Fire Force (the Great Cataclysm, then Shinra's remake),
Soul Eater (the remade world itself),
Witchblade (rebuilt Tokyo after the Great Quake),
AKB0048 (Earth ruined, humanity fled, music banned),
Neon Genesis Evangelion (Second Impact),
Dr. Stone (petrification → rebuild),
Nausicaä (Seven Days of Fire),
Castle in the Sky (fallen sky civilization),
From the New World (fallen modern civilization),
Sailor Moon (fallen Moon Kingdom, backstory).
In every one, the present lives *below* a more advanced past — ruins,
walls, domes, frontier towns, or a home planet left behind.

**Hidden history — someone erased or controls the past:**
One Piece (the Void Century: 100 years erased, reading Poneglyphs is a
crime, Ohara destroyed for it), Attack on Titan (the royal government hid
the outside world and edited memory), Psycho-Pass (Sibyl's nature hidden),
Ghost in the Shell (memories hackable and editable), Ergo Proxy (the
city's past concealed), Legend of the Galactic Heroes (official histories
vs. what happened), Neon Genesis Evangelion (SEELE's plan hidden),
Castle in the Sky (the sky civilization hidden),
From the New World (the past hidden from its own children),
Bleach (the Soul Society's existence and history concealed from the
living world; its true rulers and history restricted).

**Just setting — no fall, no hiding, just the era the story picked:**
Inuyasha, Kenshin, Champloo, Rose of Versailles, Initial D, K-On!,
Death Note, Demon Slayer, JoJo, the school shows, the modern city shows,
Digimon Adventure (1999).
These aren't resets. They're just set when they're set.

## Why this matters for the real-history version of your question

Fiction exaggerates two mechanisms that are genuinely documented in real
history, at smaller scale:

- **Collapse is real.** The Bronze Age collapse (~1200 BCE) took down palace
  civilizations across the Mediterranean; trade, writing, and city life
  shrank for centuries in places. Rome's fall did the same regionally —
  concrete, roads, and literacy thinned out. Societies do fall and restart
  lower. That's not mythology, it's archaeology.
- **Concealment is real.** Spanish priests burned Maya codices in 1562.
  Qin Shi Huang ordered books burned. Colonial administrations erased
  records and languages. Institutions do hide history — your October
  position (mechanism, not mythology) holds here too.

What fiction does is scale both up to the whole world at once: one fall
that takes *everyone* down, one erasure that hides *everything*. Real
collapses and erasures are regional and partial — which is exactly why
they're checkable, and why the world-scale versions stay in the fiction
tier. The tech-level list makes the fictional pattern visible; the
paperwork keeps the real version honest.
