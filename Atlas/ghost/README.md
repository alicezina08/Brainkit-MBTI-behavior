# Ghost shelf — BrainKit Universal Atlas

**Created 2026-11-18. Part of the public-safe BrainKit — shared under the MIT License.**

## What this shelf is

Ten ghost types as BrainKit profiles, built so a game developer can make a game
ghost that behaves the way the stories say it behaves — a residual haunting that
replays and ignores you, a poltergeist that throws things and speaks through a
child, a White Lady who asks for a ride and vanishes at the cemetery gates.

Each record follows the Common Entity Record (14-field schema), in the same shape
as the Goetia and Mythic shelves: brain profile, personality (MBTI as a labeled
interpretation), skills (the moveset), behavior model, memory model, confidence
and conflicts, and a game-implementation note.

## The honest limit — read this first

**These profiles are LORE-GRADE (evidence_grade: `generated-from-lore`), not
measured science.** There is no verified, reproducible "captured ghost data" —
no ghost brain scan, no measured ghost behavior. Ghosts are not scientifically
proven, and this shelf does not claim otherwise.

What the shelf *is* grounded in — the most real material available:

- **Real folklore** (public-domain traditions): the Irish bean sí, the
  will-o'-the-wisp, the Dullahan, Black Shuck.
- **Famous reported cases** (named in each record): the Treasurer's House
  Roman soldiers (1953), the Brown Lady of Raynham Hall (1936 photograph),
  the Enfield Poltergeist (1977–1979), Resurrection Mary (1930s–), Emilie
  Sagée's double (1845), the Bungay black dog (1577).
- **Documented modern reports**: the Hat Man (Hollis, 2010; documentary, 2019),
  the Brown Mountain Lights (reported since 1913; USGS, 1922).
- **Alice's own firsthand accounts** (`firsthand-accounts.md`, shared 2026-11-18):
  her childhood home (a Teddy Ruxpin speaking with no batteries, no tape, and its
  cords gutted; a blanket levitating a foot above her; fans forming voices), the
  next house (men's voices and groups arguing in her sister's dark room with no
  radio and no TV, heard by Alice and her sister for an hour), and the graveyard
  stairs (her brother, mother, and father watching her fly up as if pushed, then
  flipped and set back on the steps safely). Recorded in her words, private, and
  the same honesty rule applies: firsthand reports, not measured data, not proof.

Each record's `confidence_and_conflicts` states the alternative explanations
and the contested status where one exists (Enfield's faked incidents, the
Brown Lady photo's disputed authenticity, sleep paralysis as the Hat Man's
leading alternative, marsh gas for the wisp). Contradictions are kept, never
silently merged — per Alice's Atlas discipline.

No "captured data" is invented anywhere in this shelf. Where a record has no
memory claim in the lore, it says so as an honest gap.

## Files

- `ghost_flagships.json` — all 10 records (combined file).
- `batches/ghost_a.json` — records 1–5 (bare array).
- `batches/ghost_b.json` — records 6–10 (bare array).
- `firsthand-accounts.md` — Alice's own firsthand accounts, in her words (shared 2026-11-18). Source material that grounds the profiles; private, same honesty rule as the rest of the shelf.

Layout matches the Goetia and Mythic shelves: combined file at the shelf root,
batches as bare arrays underneath.

## Why this exists

Alice's direction (2026-11-18), after Hideo Kojima's idea of putting a real
captured ghost into a game: since no captured ghost data exists, the closest
real thing is the folklore and the famous reported cases — so game ghosts get
profiles grounded in those, labeled honestly as lore, instead of invented
from nothing. The game doesn't need the ghost to be scientifically proven;
it needs a personality grounded in the stories people actually tell.
